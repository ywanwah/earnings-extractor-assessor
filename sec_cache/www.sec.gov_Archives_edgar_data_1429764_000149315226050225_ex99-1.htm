BLINK CHARGING ANNOUNCES THIRD QUARTER 2026 FINANCIAL RESULTS

 

●Q3 Revenue of $18.4 million, reflecting continued portfolio rationalization and timing of project deliveries.
●GAAP operating loss of $6.9 million, compared with $6.2 million in the prior quarter.
●Gross Margin of 26.3%, driven by non-cash inventory charge; Non-GAAP gross margin of 55.0%, an expansion of over 1,400 basis points year-over-year.
●Achieved first-ever positive quarterly Adjusted EBITDA of $0.2 million, one quarter ahead of target,
●Non-GAAP operating expenses decreased 46% year-over-year to $10.0 million, reflecting continued cost discipline and operating efficiencies.
●Expanded EnergyConnectTM deployment to 52 Blink-owned sites; initial reduction of 21% in demand fees across high-capacity sites.
●Ended the quarter with approximately $30.5 million in cash and cash equivalents and no debt.
●Mike Battaglia appointed Chairman of the Board, while continuing as CEO, strengthening strategic alignment and leadership continuity as Blink Energy enters its next phase of growth.

THIRD QUARTER 2026 FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS

 

Revenues

 

Total revenue for the third quarter was approximately $18.4 million, a 15.1% sequential decrease from $21.7 million in the second quarter of 2026 and a 32.0% decrease from $27.1 million in the third quarter of 2025. Revenue came in below Blink’s internal projections, primarily reflecting the timing of certain customer projects that were not completed and recognized during the quarter. Blink expects these projects to be delivered in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to customer schedules and project execution.

 

Product revenues were $6.4 million in the third quarter, a decrease of 51% year-over-year, and representing approximately 35% of total revenue.

 

Service revenue was approximately $10.0 million, compared with $10.8 million in the third quarter of 2025, a 6.9% year-over-year decrease, entirely driven by deliberate non-renewal of a large contract in Europe. Network fee revenue, a recurring component of service revenue, increased 6.3% year-over-year to approximately $2.9 million. Charging service revenue decreased 11.5% year-over-year, reflecting Blink’s continued focus on contracts with attractive margin profiles as described above.

 

Blink continues to make progress towards its long-term objective of generating approximately 80% of revenues from recurring and repeatable revenue streams, which represented approximately 55% of total revenue in the third quarter.

 

Other revenues, including warranty fees, grants and rebates, and other revenues, were approximately $1.9 million, up 1.1% sequentially.

 

Car-sharing revenues were zero in the third quarter, following the divestiture of Envoy Technologies on June 5, 2026, compared with $1.2 million in the prior-year period.

 

Excluding revenue associated with the Envoy divestiture and the deliberate non-renewal of a large contract in Europe, non-GAAP revenues in the first and the second quarters of 2026 were $18.0 million and $19.7 million, respectively, compared to $18.4 million in the third quarter of 2026. With no revenue adjustments required in the third quarter, GAAP revenue now fully reflects Blink’s core business following these portfolio realignments.

 

 

 

 

Gross Profit and Margins

 

Gross profit was $4.8 million, a 26.3% margin, compared with $8.4 million, a 38.9% margin, in the second quarter of 2026 and $9.3 million, a 34.5% margin, in the third quarter of 2025. Third quarter gross profit includes an approximately $3 million non-cash inventory adjustment, comprising a $2.2 million excess and obsolete (E&O) inventory provision and approximately $0.8 million in additional charges related to dated and obsolete inventory parts. Excluding this non-cash charge, it would have resulted in a gross margin of 41.7%.

 

Following the completion of its transition to contract manufacturing in January 2026, as previously disclosed, Blink continued to evaluate its remaining legacy manufacturing inventory throughout the year. This process included assessing opportunities to repurpose existing components through its contract manufacturing partners or monetizing inventory through third-party sales, with partial success in both areas. During the third quarter, the Company substantially completed this assessment and identified remaining inventory that could not be economically utilized or recovered, resulting in the above-mentioned non-cash inventory charge.

 

Non-GAAP gross margin, which excludes non-recurring and non-cash charges and depreciation and amortization, was 55.0%, compared with 40.9% in the third quarter of 2025.

 

Operating Expenses

 

Total operating expenses were $11.8 million, compared with $9.8 million in the third quarter of 2025. The prior-year period included an $11.7 million non-cash gain from the change in fair value of consideration payable and earn-out liabilities. Excluding that item, total operating expenses decreased 45.1% year-over-year. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $10.0 million, a 46% year-over-year reduction.

 

Cost optimization efforts continued to reduce the Company’s cost base. Compensation expenses declined 41.9% from $11.7 million in the third quarter of 2025 to $6.8 million in the third quarter of 2026. General and administrative expenses declined to approximately $1.2 million, compared with $3.7 million in the prior-year period, while other operating expenses decreased to approximately $2.8 million from $4.8 million.

 

Net Loss and Loss Per Share

 

Net loss was $6.7 million, or $(0.05) per diluted share, compared with a net loss of $0.3 million, or $(0.00) per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025. Net loss for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $24.3 million, compared with $50.7 million in the same period of 2025. The prior-year quarter benefited from the $11.7 million non-cash fair value gain noted above.

 

Adjusted Non-GAAP Net Earnings per share for the third quarter of 2026 improved to $0.00 per share compared to a Non-GAAP Net Loss per share of $(0.07) in the third quarter of 2025, For the third quarter of 2026, Blink’s weighted average number of shares outstanding was 145.4 million, compared to 109.1 million for the third quarter of 2025.

 

Adjusted EBITDA

 

Adjusted EBITDA was $0.2 million, the first positive quarter in the Company’s history, compared with a loss of $(7.3) million in the third quarter of 2025, an improvement of $7.5 million. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the adjusted EBITDA loss improved 76% year-over-year to $(7.1) million from $(29.4) million. See reconciling tables below for the definitions of non-GAAP numbers referenced above.

 

 

 

 

Balance Sheet and Liquidity

 

As of September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents were approximately $30.5 million, compared with $34.0 million as of June 30, 2026, a decrease of approximately $3.5 million in the quarter. Blink continues to invest in high-quality DC fast charging infrastructure and energy management solutions, such as the EnergyConnect platform.

 

Business Outlook

 

2026 is an inflection year for Blink as the Company completes its operational transformation. Blink is updating its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $78 million to $81 million from previously guided $83 million to $90 million. The revised outlook primarily reflects the Company’s continued focus on revenue quality.

 

Blink continues to expect full-year 2026 gross margin of approximately 38%, excluding non-cash inventory adjustment.

 

Blink achieved positive adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter, one quarter ahead of its target to reach adjusted EBITDA breakeven. Blink remains focused on achieving sustainable adjusted EBITDA profitability as it enters the final quarter of 2026, supported by its streamlined cost structure and continued operational discipline. Looking ahead, the Company expects to return to revenue growth in 2027, driven by its evolving business model, expanding repeat and recurring revenue opportunities, and the strategic evolution into energy management solutions. Blink intends to provide formal 2027 financial guidance in conjunction with its full-year 2026 financial results.

 

Board Matters

 

On November 3, 2026, the Board of Directors (the “Board”) of the Company appointed Michael Battaglia, the Company’s Chief Executive Officer and a member of the Board, to serve as Chairman of the Board, effective immediately. Mr. Battaglia succeeds Ritsaart van Montfrans, who has served as Chairman of the Board since June 2023. Mr. Battaglia will continue to serve as the Company’s Chief Executive Officer and, in connection with his appointment as Chairman, will no longer hold the title of President.

 

Mr. van Montfrans will continue to serve as a member of the Board and has been appointed by the Board to serve as Lead Independent Director, effective immediately. As Lead Independent Director, Mr. van Montfrans will, among other things, preside over executive sessions of the independent directors and serve as a liaison between the independent directors and the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer.

 

Blink’s board of directors believes that combining the roles of Chairman and Chief Executive Officer under Mr. Battaglia provides unified leadership and clear accountability for the execution of the Company’s strategy, while the Lead Independent Director role maintains strong independent oversight of management.

Blink changed the definitions of its non-GAAP reporting measures in the first quarter of 2026 to align better with its peers and industry standards. Reconciliation tables are presented above.

 

Non-GAAP Revenues is defined as GAAP total revenues adjusted to exclude revenues attributable to (i) Envoy Technologies, which Blink divested on June 5, 2026, and (ii) a material contract in Europe that was not renewed, each as previously disclosed with the Company’s second quarter 2026 results. Blink Charging believes Non-GAAP Revenues provides investors with a clearer, period-over-period comparable view of revenues from the Company’s continuing core business following these portfolio exit actions. No revenue adjustments were required in the third quarter of 2026.

 

Non-GAAP Gross Profit is defined as GAAP gross profit adjusted to exclude (i) depreciation and amortization charges included in cost of revenues, and (ii) non-recurring or non-cash charges within cost of revenues (such as inventory write-downs or one-time warranty costs). Blink Charging believes Non-GAAP Gross Profit provides investors with a clearer view of the Company’s underlying operational profitability by removing the impact of asset depreciation related to its charging infrastructure build-out and non-recurring items that are not indicative of ongoing performance. Non-GAAP Gross Margin is Non-GAAP Gross Profit divided by total revenues.

 

Non-GAAP Operating Expenses is defined as GAAP total operating expenses adjusted to exclude (i) stock-based compensation, (ii) depreciation and amortization within operating expenses, (iii) non-recurring and non-cash charges (including severance and retention payments, executive recruiting fees, one-time legal and consulting costs, and charges related to discontinued software or services), and (iv) other adjustments. Blink Charging believes Non-GAAP Operating Expenses is a useful measure for investors to assess the Company’s structural cost base and ongoing operating expense discipline, as it removes the impact of non-cash compensation, asset depreciation, and one-time charges that do not reflect recurring operational costs.

 

Non-GAAP Net Loss excludes stock-based compensation, non-recurring and non-cash charges, and changes in fair value of consideration payable, but unlike Adjusted EBITDA, retains the impact of depreciation and amortization within operating expenses and interest income/expense. See “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” for a full reconciliation.

 

 

 

 

Adjusted EBITDA is defined as Non-GAAP Net Loss adjusted to add back: (i) provision for income taxes; (ii) depreciation and amortization within operating expenses; less (iii) net interest and other income (expense). This reconciliation bridge corresponds directly to the line items presented in the Non-GAAP reconciliation tables above.

 

Blink Charging believes Adjusted EBITDA is useful to management, securities analysts, and investors to evaluate the Company’s core operating performance because it removes the impact of non-cash charges, non-recurring items, financing activity, taxes, and capital investment depreciation that are not indicative of the Company’s recurring operational results. Adjusted EBITDA should be considered in addition to, and not as a substitute for, Net Loss or other measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP.

 

Our definition of Adjusted EBITDA and Non-GAAP EPS may differ from other companies reporting similarly named measures. These measures should be considered in addition to, and not as a substitute for, or superior to, other measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP, such as Net Loss, and Diluted Earnings per Share.

 

Non-GAAP Adjusted EPS is defined as GAAP net loss per diluted share adjusted to exclude, on a per-share basis, the same non-cash and non-recurring items used in the Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation: (i) stock based compensation, (ii) non-recurring and non-cash charges, (iii) change in fair value related to consideration payable, (iv) provision for income taxes, (v) interest expense, and (vi) depreciation and amortization. Blink Charging believes Non-GAAP EPS is a useful supplemental measure for investors as it provides a per-share view of the Company’s core operating performance on a basis consistent with Adjusted EBITDA, excluding non-cash and non-recurring items that management does not consider reflective of the Company’s ongoing operations. Non-GAAP EPS should not be confused with GAAP diluted EPS and should be considered in addition to, and not as a substitute for, GAAP diluted earnings (loss) per share.

 

Investors should be aware that non-GAAP financial measures have inherent limitations. In particular, certain adjustments to Blink’s GAAP results — such as stock-based compensation — are recurring in nature and are expected to continue for the foreseeable future; stock-based compensation is a meaningful component of employee compensation and plays an important role in Blink’s ability to attract, retain, and motivate its workforce. In addition, Blink’s non-GAAP measures are not calculated pursuant to any standardized GAAP methodology, and the specific items Blink excludes may differ from those excluded by other companies presenting similarly titled non-GAAP measures, which may limit comparability. Blink may also, in future periods, exclude additional items it determines are not reflective of its core operating performance.
