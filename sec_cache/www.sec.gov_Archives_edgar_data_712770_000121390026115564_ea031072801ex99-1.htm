ONE LIBERTY PROPERTIES REPORTS

THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS

 

– Rental Income Increases 11.9% Year Over Year –

– Acquired Industrial Building for $11.3 Million and Under Contract to Acquire a $34 Million Industrial Portfolio –

– Realized $30.7 Million in Net Proceeds from Sales During and Subsequent to the Quarter –

– Industrial Properties Represent Approximately 86% of Base Rent –

 

GREAT NECK, New York, November 3, 2026 — One Liberty Properties, Inc. (NYSE: OLP), a real estate investment trust focused on the ownership of industrial properties, today announced operating results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.

 

“Third quarter results reflect the continued growth of our industrial portfolio, with total revenues up 11.9% from the prior year, driven by the contribution from our accretive industrial acquisitions,” stated Patrick J. Callan, Jr., President and Chief Executive Officer of One Liberty. “We continue to recycle capital from retail and other assets that have reached peak value into industrial properties, which now represent approximately 86% of our base rent. We were also pleased to acquire a 100,000 square foot industrial building in Florida, adding another high-quality asset in the Southeast, a region with sustained demand. We also entered into an agreement to acquire a four-building industrial portfolio in the Milwaukee MSA, that, upon closing, will add another diverse tenant base in a tight industrial submarket. With our new credit facility in place, we remain well-positioned to continue executing on our industrial growth strategy and creating long-term value for our stockholders.”

 

Third Quarter and Recent Highlights:

 

●Net income of $0.04 per diluted share, compared to $0.48 in the third quarter of 2025, due principally to a $887,000 net gain on sales of real estate in the current quarter versus a $9.1 million net gain on sales of real estate in the prior-year quarter.

 

●7.1% growth in FFO1 per diluted share to $0.45 per share, up $0.03 from the third quarter of 2025, and 8.7% growth in AFFO per diluted share1 to $0.50 per share, up $0.04 from the third quarter of 2025, driven primarily by the increase in rental income.

 

●Total revenues of $26.6 million, up $2.8 million, or 11.9%, from the third quarter of 2025.

 

●Industrial properties represent approximately 86% of base rent, and portfolio occupancy was 97.5% as of quarter end.

 

●Entered into a new $100 million revolving credit facility, extending maturity and enhancing financial flexibility, as previously disclosed.

 

●Acquired a 100,000 square foot industrial building in Ocala, Florida for $11.3 million.

 

●Signed a definitive agreement to purchase a four-building, 277,000 square foot industrial portfolio in the Milwaukee, Wisconsin MSA for $34 million, with closing expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.

●Sold two non-core retail properties for an aggregate price of $7.8 million, generating net proceeds of $6.2 million and a net gain, before a $1.4 million impairment loss, of $887,000.

 

●Sold five properties, including two industrial and three retail, for an aggregate price of $25.1 million and aggregate net proceeds of approximately $24.5 million subsequent to quarter end.

 

Key Drivers of Third Quarter Results:

 

●Rental income, net, grew 11.9%, or $2.8 million, year over year due primarily to accretive acquisitions, which contributed an additional $3.4 million, partially offset by $661,000 from properties sold.

 

●Total operating expenses were $19.3 million compared to $17.0 million year over year, primarily due to a $1.6 million increase in depreciation and amortization and higher real estate expenses related to additional industrial properties acquired.

 

●Interest expense was $6.8 million compared to $5.6 million in the prior year quarter, reflecting an increase in the weighted average principal amount of mortgage debt outstanding and, to a lesser extent, higher weighted average interest rates.

 

●FFO grew 9.7% to $9.9 million, and AFFO increased 9.3% to $11.0 million, driven primarily by an increase in rental income related to accretive industrial acquisitions, offset primarily by additional interest expense.

 

●Diluted per share net income, FFO and AFFO were impacted compared to the corresponding quarter in the prior year by an average increase of approximately 224,000 in the weighted average number of shares of common stock outstanding as a result of stock issuances in connection with the equity incentive and dividend reinvestment programs.

At September 30, 2026, the Company had $20.1 million of cash and cash equivalents, total assets of $880.5 million, total debt of $543.4 million (including $10.0 million outstanding under its credit facility), and total OLP stockholders’ equity of $297.1 million.

At October 30, 2026, One Liberty’s available liquidity was $104.8 million, including $21.0 million of cash and cash equivalents (including the credit facility’s required $3.0 million average deposit maintenance balance) and $83.8 million available under its credit facility.

 

As previously disclosed, in July, the Company and its lenders entered into a new $100 million credit facility with a scheduled maturity in December 2029 and an extension right to December 2030. Interest is based on the 30-day SOFR plus an applicable margin, based on the ratio of total debt to the value of the Company’s properties, of between 175 and 250 basis points. The new facility adds an “accordion” feature which provides the option to increase the facility by up to $50 million, and it expands the purposes for which the facility can be used, further enhancing the Company’s financial flexibility.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures:

 

One Liberty computes funds from operations, or FFO, in accordance with the “White Paper on Funds From Operations” issued by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts (“NAREIT”) and NAREIT’s related guidance. FFO is defined in the White Paper as net income (calculated in accordance with GAAP), excluding depreciation and amortization related to real estate, gains and losses from the sale of certain real estate assets, gains and losses from change in control, impairment write-downs of certain real estate assets and investments in entities where the impairment is directly attributable to decreases in the value of depreciable real estate held by the entity. Adjustments for unconsolidated partnerships and joint ventures are calculated to reflect FFO on the same basis. In computing FFO, the Company does not add back to net income the amortization of costs in connection with its financing activities or depreciation of non-real estate assets.

 

3

 

One Liberty computes adjusted funds from operations, or AFFO, by adjusting from FFO for straight-line rent accruals and amortization of lease intangibles, deducting from income (i) additional rent from a ground lease tenant, (ii) income on settlement of litigation, (iii) income on insurance recoveries from casualties, (iv) lease termination and assignment fees, and adding back to income (i) amortization of restricted stock and restricted stock unit compensation expense, (ii) amortization of costs in connection with its financing activities (including its share of its unconsolidated joint ventures), (iii) debt prepayment costs, (iv) amortization of lease incentives and (v) mortgage intangible assets. Since the NAREIT White Paper does not provide guidelines for computing AFFO, the computation of AFFO varies from one REIT to another.

 

One Liberty believes that FFO and AFFO are useful and standard supplemental measures of the operating performance for equity REITs and are used frequently by securities analysts, investors and other interested parties in evaluating equity REITs, many of which present FFO and AFFO when reporting their operating results. FFO and AFFO are intended to exclude GAAP historical cost depreciation and amortization of real estate assets, which assumes that the value of real estate assets diminish predictability over time. In fact, real estate values have historically risen and fallen with market conditions. As a result, the Company believes that FFO and AFFO provide a performance measure that when compared year over year, should reflect the impact to operations from trends in occupancy rates, rental rates, operating costs, interest costs and other matters without the inclusion of depreciation and amortization, providing a perspective that may not be necessarily apparent from net income. Management also considers FFO and AFFO to be useful in evaluating potential property acquisitions.

 

FFO and AFFO do not represent net income or cash flows from operations as defined by GAAP. FFO and AFFO and should not be considered to be an alternative to net income as a reliable measure of One Liberty’s operating performance; nor should FFO and AFFO be considered an alternative to cash flows from operating, investing or financing activities (as defined by GAAP) as measures of liquidity. FFO and AFFO do not measure whether cash flow is sufficient to fund all of the Company’s cash needs, including principal amortization, capital improvements and distributions to stockholders. Management recognizes that there are limitations in the use of FFO and AFFO. In evaluating One Liberty’s performance, management is careful to examine GAAP measures such as net income and cash flows from operating, investing and financing activities.
